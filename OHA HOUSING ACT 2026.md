# OHA HOUSING ACT 2026

**OBINexus Federation/Confederation — Proposed Model Legislation**  
**Draft for consultation — 9 October 2026**  
**Originator: OBINexus Nnamdi Michael Okpala**

**Status:** This document proposes rules for an OBINexus community housing programme. It is not presented as enacted Nigerian or United Kingdom legislation. Adoption by OBINexus establishes programme commitments only to the extent lawfully incorporated into its governing documents, agreements and operations; it does not itself establish sovereign jurisdiction or bind governments, courts or third parties.

**Long title:** An Act to establish a community framework for safe, accessible and independent housing; to prevent and relieve homelessness, with particular protection for neurodivergent persons; to provide housing and essential services through the OHA–IWU–IJI framework; to protect dignity, consent and freedom of choice; and to provide for funding, accountability and remedies.

**Proposed enactment formula:** Be it adopted by the OBINexus Assembly, for participating community housing programmes and subject to applicable law, as follows.

## PART I — PRELIMINARY

### Section 1 — Short title

This instrument may be cited as the **OHA Housing Act 2026**.

### Section 2 — Adoption and commencement

1. This Act takes effect within a participating programme on the date recorded in a published adoption resolution.
2. The resolution must identify the responsible legal entity, participating sites, approved budget and arrangements for implementing this Act.
3. No adoption shall be backdated. The title year records the proposal's origin and does not establish an earlier commencement date.
4. Before accepting applications, a programme must appoint its housing authority and independent review panel, publish contact details and establish emergency assistance arrangements.

### Section 3 — Interpretation

In this Act:

- **OHA** means the community pillar: belonging, participation and shared responsibility for meeting essential needs.
- **IWU** means the rules and accountability pillar: published standards, fair decisions and review.
- **IJI** means the order pillar: maintaining order to mitigate chaos through prevention, maintenance, de-escalation and proportionate responses.
- **Authority** means the community housing body appointed under section 8.
- **Assembly** means the body authorised by the participating organisation's governing documents to adopt and oversee this Act.
- **Independent housing** means a home in which a resident controls ordinary daily life, privacy, access and personal choices, with support where requested.
- **Neurodivergent person** means a person whose cognitive, developmental or neurological functioning differs from commonly assumed patterns, including autistic persons and persons with ADHD or learning differences.
- **Homelessness** includes having no safe accommodation available or having accommodation that cannot reasonably be occupied because of violence, serious danger or essential-service failure.
- **Resident** includes a person occupying programme housing under a tenancy, licence or other lawful occupancy agreement.
- **Participating programme** means a housing scheme operated by an identified legal entity that has formally adopted this Act.

### Section 4 — Purpose and scope

1. The purpose of this Act is to ensure that people can obtain and retain a safe home and live with dignity, autonomy and community belonging.
2. Its long-term objective is to end homelessness within participating communities, with particular attention to neurodivergent persons and people excluded from existing provision.
3. Duties under this Act apply to participating authorities, operators, staff and contractors through their lawful governing and contractual arrangements.
4. This Act does not authorise occupation of another person's land, compulsory acquisition, detention, policing or the displacement of applicable law.

## PART II — HOUSING RIGHTS

### Section 5 — Right to housing assistance

1. Every applicant has a right under the programme to an accessible application process, an individual assessment, written reasons and independent review.
2. An eligible person shall receive a documented housing offer or an assistance plan, including interim protection where immediate danger exists.
3. The Authority shall actively seek suitable housing and shall not treat placing a person on a waiting list as sufficient assistance.
4. A lack of available permanent housing must be recorded honestly, together with interim arrangements, responsible officers and a review date.

### Section 6 — Equality and autonomy

1. Housing decisions must not discriminate on grounds of neurodivergence, disability, ethnicity, sex, religion or other personal characteristics unrelated to a lawful eligibility requirement.
2. A diagnosis, speech difficulty, unconventional communication or request for support shall not by itself be treated as evidence of inability to live independently.
3. Housing shall not depend on marriage, parenthood, political loyalty, religious participation, unpaid labour or participation in research.
4. A resident may work, study, create, rest, receive visitors and pursue personal interests, subject to proportionate rules protecting other residents' rights.
5. Residents remain free to leave the programme, subject to ordinary lawful notice obligations; programme membership shall not restrict freedom of movement.

### Section 7 — Consent and healthcare choices

1. Acceptance or retention of housing shall not be conditional on taking medication, accepting a diagnosis or undergoing treatment.
2. Programme staff shall respect informed choices to accept or refuse healthcare and shall offer accessible information and voluntary support.
3. Nothing in this Act creates a power to compel treatment or overrides applicable healthcare law.
4. Housing staff shall not advise residents to stop prescribed treatment or substitute housing decisions for clinical advice.
5. Support must be individually agreed and reviewed. Consent to housing is separate from consent to care, research, surveillance or publication of personal information.

## PART III — GOVERNANCE

### Section 8 — Community Housing Authority: OHA

1. The Assembly shall establish an Authority accountable to residents and the participating legal entity.
2. The Authority shall assess applications, arrange accommodation, manage repairs, coordinate essential services and administer housing funds.
3. Residents shall elect representatives, including neurodivergent residents, to participate in its oversight.
4. Officers must disclose conflicts of interest and withdraw from decisions concerning themselves, relatives or financial interests.

### Section 9 — Rules and independent review: IWU

1. The Authority shall publish eligibility criteria, allocation rules, charges, occupancy terms and complaint procedures in accessible formats.
2. An independent Housing Review Panel shall hear challenges to eligibility, allocation, charges, suitability, termination and failures to provide assistance.
3. No person involved in the original decision may determine its review.
4. The Panel shall operate under section 22 and shall not describe itself as a state court.

### Section 10 — Order and prevention: IJI

1. IJI shall maintain order to mitigate chaos by coordinating building safety, maintenance, emergency planning, conflict prevention and de-escalation.
2. Responses must address specific conduct or hazards, use the least restrictive effective measure and preserve dignity.
3. A diagnosis, disagreement or refusal of medication is not a safety violation.
4. IJI personnel have no independent authority to detain, punish, search or confiscate property. Emergency incidents shall be referred to appropriate lawful services when necessary.

## PART IV — ACCESS AND ALLOCATION

### Section 11 — Accessible applications

1. A person may request housing by email, letter, telephone, an accessible online form, in person or through a chosen representative.
2. Requests may use speech, writing, symbols, assistive technology or another usable communication method.
3. No particular wording, legal citation or diagnosis certificate is required to make an initial request.
4. Staff shall help applicants complete necessary information. Inability to complete a standard form is not grounds for rejection.

### Section 12 — Assessment and decisions

1. Applications shall be acknowledged within two working days and assessed within ten working days.
2. A decision or reasoned interim update shall be provided within fifteen working days of the initial request.
3. Assessment must consider safety, present housing, accessibility, sensory needs, household circumstances, income and the applicant's preferences.
4. Where information is missing, the Authority must explain what is needed, offer assistance and assess urgent needs without waiting for routine documents.
5. Every decision must name the responsible officer, give reasons and explain review rights.

### Section 13 — Emergency protection

1. A report of immediate homelessness, violence or dangerous accommodation requires a same-day risk assessment.
2. Where urgent protection is needed, the Authority shall arrange safe interim accommodation or activate a documented referral and assistance agreement without delay.
3. Emergency assistance shall not be withheld solely because permanent eligibility remains unresolved.
4. Interim placements must include access to drinking water, sanitation, food assistance where needed, and a route to longer-term housing.

### Section 14 — Fair allocation

1. Published allocation rules shall prioritise immediate danger, homelessness, serious unmet accessibility needs and threats to housing stability.
2. Eligible applicants in equivalent circumstances shall receive offers according to a published and auditable tie-breaking procedure.
3. An unsuitable offer may be challenged without automatic loss of priority.
4. A pending application records a request for assistance; it does not itself transfer property ownership or guarantee an immediately available dwelling.

## PART V — HOMES AND RESIDENT SUPPORT

### Section 15 — Minimum housing standards

1. Every home shall have lawful occupancy arrangements, structural safety, weather protection, ventilation, sanitation, safe drinking water and appropriate cooking facilities.
2. Electrical installations, fire precautions and building works must meet applicable standards and inspection requirements.
3. Layouts and adaptations shall address individual mobility, sensory, communication and privacy requirements.
4. Shared facilities shall not deprive residents of a secure private living space. Shared bedrooms shall not be imposed solely because a person is neurodivergent.
5. A dangerous defect requires immediate protective action; other repairs require a published response timetable and escalation route.

### Section 16 — Independent living and reablement

1. Residents may request help with budgeting, meals, cleaning, communication, education, employment or daily living.
2. Support shall build or sustain independence according to the resident's goals, rather than require conformity to one lifestyle.
3. Progress reviews shall examine whether support is useful, not whether the resident obeys personal preferences of staff.
4. Declining optional support shall not itself justify termination of housing.

### Section 16A — MMUCO physical operating systems

1. A participating MMUCO site shall coordinate homes, water, food access, sanitation, energy, maintenance and voluntary support as a physical system for sustaining everyday life.
2. A site may use the name MMUCO ORION or another published programme name. Its name does not alter the operator's legal responsibilities.
3. Housing may include duplexes, individual homes or other suitable dwellings. The Authority shall consider the household's needs, preferences and ability to maintain the space, with assistance where requested.
4. Sites should provide accessible gardens or food-growing facilities where practicable, together with safe food storage and preparation facilities. Growing food supplements an accountable food-supply plan; crop failure must not leave residents without essential food.
5. The operator shall maintain contingency plans for water shortages, power failures, interrupted deliveries and unavailable maintenance staff.
6. Software and automation may support service coordination, but essential assistance must remain accessible when digital systems fail. Residents shall have a human contact and an offline request route.
7. Council or government funding may be recorded only where an actual grant, contract or other lawful funding arrangement exists; a proposal alone shall not be represented as a commitment to pay.
8. Where the programme uses YES, NO and MAYBE to record choices, YES means an informed agreement, NO means refusal, and MAYBE means undecided or more information needed. MAYBE and silence shall not be treated as consent. A resident may change a choice, subject to any separately agreed lawful obligations.

### Section 17 — Community participation

1. Community activities and contributions shall be voluntary, accessible and compatible with residents' abilities and circumstances.
2. No resident shall lose essential housing, food or water because they cannot work or choose not to undertake unpaid duties.
3. Paid work shall be governed by a separate lawful agreement. Housing membership does not replace employment protections.

### Section 18 — Privacy and access to homes

1. Entry requires the resident's consent and reasonable notice, except where applicable law permits emergency entry.
2. Inspections must have a stated purpose and be conducted with minimum intrusion.
3. Personal information shall be collected only for stated purposes, secured, retained only as necessary and disclosed only on an appropriate lawful basis.
4. Public reports shall protect residents' identities. Publication of a resident's story requires separate, freely given consent.

## PART VI — AFFORDABILITY AND RETENTION

### Section 19 — Community Housing Fund

1. The Authority shall maintain a ring-fenced Housing Fund for acquisition, construction, adaptations, essential services, repairs and emergency assistance.
2. Funding may include lawful grants, donations, member contributions, earned income and public partnerships.
3. The Assembly shall approve an annual budget and publish income, expenditure, obligations and funding gaps.
4. Essential homes and repairs shall take priority over promotional expenditure.
5. The Authority shall commission an annual independent financial examination proportionate to the programme's scale.

### Section 20 — Charges and rent-free provision

1. The programme shall seek to provide rent-free housing to people unable to pay, supported by the Housing Fund.
2. Any charge must be published, individually assessed, affordable and agreed before occupation; no undisclosed fee may be imposed.
3. A resident experiencing hardship shall be offered a reassessment, assistance and a realistic payment arrangement.
4. A funding shortfall must trigger a published recovery plan; it shall not be concealed through promises of funded housing that the programme cannot deliver.

### Section 21 — Prevention of eviction and displacement

1. Termination is a last resort, subject to the occupancy agreement and applicable law.
2. Before seeking termination, the Authority must consider adaptations, mediation, hardship assistance and other reasonable alternatives.
3. A resident shall receive accessible written reasons, legally sufficient notice and an opportunity for independent review.
4. Staff shall not use lockouts, removal of possessions, utility disconnection or intimidation to obtain possession.
5. Where displacement cannot lawfully be avoided, the Authority shall prepare a housing continuity plan and actively seek suitable alternative accommodation.

## PART VII — REMEDIES AND ACCOUNTABILITY

### Section 22 — Complaints and review

1. Complaints may be made by any method permitted under section 11, without a fee.
2. A resident or applicant may request review within thirty days of a decision. Late requests shall be accepted where communication barriers, illness or other reasonable circumstances explain the delay.
3. Urgent reviews concerning imminent danger or displacement shall be considered within forty-eight hours; other reviews shall be determined within twenty working days.
4. The person may use a chosen advocate, submit evidence and receive an accessible explanation of the outcome.
5. Under the programme's adopted governing arrangements, the Panel may require reconsideration, reassessment, repairs, correction of charges or restoration of assistance. Financial remedies must have a lawful basis and identified funding.
6. A complaint shall not result in retaliation. Programme review does not remove access to external lawful remedies.

### Section 23 — Reporting and implementation

1. The Authority shall publish quarterly reports on applications, waiting times, placements, homelessness prevention, repairs, complaints and unresolved needs.
2. Within ninety days of adoption, it shall publish a delivery plan naming responsible officers, funded actions and measurable milestones.
3. Any missed duty or deadline shall be recorded with its cause, immediate mitigation and corrective action date.
4. Residents shall participate in an annual review of delivery and a review of this Act at least every two years.

### Section 24 — Regulations and amendment

1. The Assembly may adopt operational rules consistent with this Act, after accessible consultation with residents.
2. Operational rules shall not remove equality, consent, privacy or review protections.
3. Amendments must be published with reasons and a commencement date. They shall not retrospectively erase unresolved complaints or established contractual rights.
