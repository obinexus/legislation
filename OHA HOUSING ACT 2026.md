# OHA HOUSING ACT 2026

**OBINexus Federation — Model Legislation**

**Long Title:** An Act to establish the right to independent housing for neurodivergent persons within the OBINexus jurisdiction; to end homelessness among neurodivergent people; to provide for community/public housing under the OHA-IWU-IJI framework; to protect the right to refuse medication; and for connected purposes.

**BE IT ENACTED** by the OBINexus Assembly, in accordance with the OHA-IWU-IJI Constitutional Framework, and by the authority of the OBINexus Neurodivergent Sovereign Declaration:

---

## PART I — PRELIMINARY

### Section 1. Short Title
This Act may be cited as the **OHA Housing Act 2026**.

### Section 2. Commencement
This Act comes into force on the date declared by the OBINexus Assembly, or on 3 October 2026, whichever is earlier.

### Section 3. Interpretation
In this Act:

- **“OHA”** means *ọha* — community, public, or the people.
- **“OBINexus”** means the federation/confederation of neurodivergent persons and recognised communities operating under the OHA-IWU-IJI framework.
- **“Neurodivergent person”** means any person whose mind, cognition, sensory processing, communication, learning, or emotional regulation differs from dominant neurological norms, including autistic, ADHD, dyslexic, dyspraxic, Tourettic, and other neurodivergent persons, whether diagnosed or self-identified.
- **“Independent housing”** means safe, accessible, sensory-considerate housing that respects autonomy, privacy, and the right to refuse medication or treatment.
- **“OHA Housing Authority”** means the body established under Section 16.
- **“Federation/Confederation”** means the FIFA-like governance model in which autonomous communities join together while retaining internal self-governance.
- **“Support”** means health, social welfare, personal assistance, communication, and practical assistance, not sickness-based treatment.
- **“Medication”** includes psychiatric drugs, depot injections, and any chemical restraint.

### Section 4. Application
This Act applies to all neurodivergent persons within OBINexus territories, recognised communities, and diaspora networks who voluntarily accept OBINexus jurisdiction.

---

## PART II — OBJECTIVES AND PRINCIPLES

### Section 5. Objectives
The objectives of this Act are:

1. To ensure that no neurodivergent person is homeless.
2. To provide independent housing as a right, not a privilege.
3. To replace sickness-based frameworks with a neurodivergent framework.
4. To protect the right to refuse medication and forced treatment.
5. To empower neurodivergent people to govern their own housing through OHA, IWU, and IJI.
6. To build systems that sustain everyone forever.

### Section 6. Guiding Principles
The following principles must guide all decisions under this Act:

1. **OHA** — community protection and public good.
2. **IWU** — law and policy must serve neurodivergent flourishing.
3. **IJI** — order and governance must be equitable and transparent.
4. **Nothing about us without us** — neurodivergent people must lead.
5. **Breathing is not optional. Living is not optional. Work is optional.**
6. **When systems fail, build your own.**

---

## PART III — THE RIGHT TO HOUSING

### Section 7. Right to Independent Housing
Every neurodivergent person has the right to independent housing.

### Section 8. Prohibition of Homelessness
No neurodivergent person shall be homeless within the OBINexus jurisdiction. Homelessness is a violation of this Act.

### Section 9. Prohibition of Forced Institutionalisation
No person shall be placed in institutional care, hospital, or congregate facility as a substitute for housing without their free and informed consent.

### Section 10. Right to Refuse Medication
No neurodivergent person shall be required to take medication, receive depot injections, or undergo any treatment as a condition of housing, support, or community membership.

### Section 11. Support, Not Sickness
Health and social welfare support shall be provided on a support-needs basis. Neurodivergence shall not be framed as sickness, disease, disorder, or defect.

### Section 12. Breathing and Living Rights
Nothing in this Act limits the Section 204 Declaration or the right to breathing, living, and ontological autonomy.

---

## PART IV — APPLICATION AND ELIGIBILITY

### Section 13. Eligibility
A person is eligible for housing under this Act if they:

1. Self-identify as neurodivergent; and
2. Are resident in, or connected to, an OBINexus recognised community; and
3. Require housing or housing support.

### Section 14. Application by Email, Letter, or Accessible Format
An application may be made by email, letter, post, typed message, tapped message, or any accessible format. Speaking is not required.

### Section 15. Duty to Acknowledge and Decide
The OHA Housing Authority must acknowledge an application within 7 days and decide within 28 days. Urgent cases must receive interim housing immediately.

### Section 16. Priority Need
Priority shall be given to:

1. Homeless persons.
2. Care leavers aged 18–25.
3. Persons fleeing abuse, coercion, or forced medication.
4. Persons without independent housing.
5. Persons in unsuitable or unsafe housing.

---

## PART V — DUTIES OF THE OHA HOUSING AUTHORITY

### Section 17. Establishment of the OHA Housing Authority
There is established the **OHA Housing Authority** to administer this Act.

### Section 18. Duty to Provide Housing
The OHA Housing Authority must acquire land, build, lease, purchase, or otherwise provide independent housing for eligible persons.

### Section 19. Duty to Maintain a Housing Register
The Authority must maintain a register of available housing, applicants, and allocations.

### Section 20. Duty to Communicate Accessibly
All communications must be available in accessible formats, including plain language, easy-read, audio, and alternative communication.

### Section 21. Duty to Fund
There is established the **OHA Housing Fund** to receive contributions, donations, grants, and community resources for housing.

---

## PART VI — HOUSING STANDARDS

### Section 22. Minimum Standards
Housing provided under this Act must be:

1. Independent or self-contained.
2. Safe and secure.
3. Sensory-considerate.
4. Accessible.
5. Connected to utilities and internet.
6. Free from forced medication or treatment.

### Section 23. Community Housing Models
Housing may be provided through:

1. Individual homes.
2. Cooperatives.
3. Community land trusts.
4. Cluster homes.
5. Supported living, only where chosen.

---

## PART VII — ENFORCEMENT AND REMEDIES

### Section 24. Enforcement by Email or Letter
A person may enforce their rights by sending an email or letter citing the relevant section of this Act. The Authority must respond within 14 days.

### Section 25. OHA Housing Tribunal
There is established the **OHA Housing Tribunal** to hear disputes under this Act.

### Section 26. Remedies
The Tribunal may order:

1. Housing provision.
2. Compensation.
3. Declaratory relief.
4. Injunctions.
5. Any other remedy necessary to protect rights.

### Section 27. Human Rights Damages
Where a right under this Act is violated, the Tribunal may award constitutional damages of not less than £1,000,000 per violation, or the equivalent in local currency.

### Section 28. Appeals
A person may appeal a Tribunal decision to the OBINexus Constitutional Court.

---

## PART VIII — GOVERNANCE

### Section 29. Federation/Confederation Autonomy
Recognised communities may adopt stricter housing protections. They may not reduce the rights in this Act.

### Section 30. External Recognition
The OBINexus Assembly may send this Act to human rights bodies, governments, and international institutions for recognition. Recognition does not replace OBINexus enforcement.

### Section 31. Amendment
This Act may be amended by the OBINexus Assembly in accordance with OHA-IWU-IJI.

### Section 32. Review
This Act shall be reviewed every 3 years.

---

## PART IX — TRANSITIONAL AND SAVINGS

### Section 33. Existing Rights Preserved
Nothing in this Act reduces existing rights under the OBINexus Neurodivergent Sovereign Declaration.

### Section 34. Commencement
This Act commences on 3 October 2026.
