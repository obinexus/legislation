## XIII. Constitutional Institutions of OBINexus

The Republic of OBINexus shall organise public responsibility through three constitutional institutions:

**OHA — Housing and Civic Provision**  
**IWU — Law, Rights and Public Administration**  
**IJI — Review, Balance and Accountability**

These institutions are separate in function but required to cooperate.

No institution may use the powers of another simply because doing so is convenient.

---

# Article 11 — OHA: Housing and Civic Provision

OHA shall protect the material foundation required for independent life.

Its duties shall include:

- access to safe and stable housing;
- protection of the home as private civic space;
- prevention of avoidable homelessness;
- accessible physical infrastructure;
- coordination with health care and reablement where necessary;
- protection of independent living.

Housing shall remain legally distinct from health care and reablement.

A person shall not become subject to medical control merely because they require housing.

A person shall not lose housing merely because they question, refuse, appeal or seek review of a separate service.

The purpose of OHA is stability.

**OHA gives the person somewhere from which life can continue.**

---

# Article 12 — Health Care

Health care shall exist for health.

It shall not be treated as a mechanism of housing allocation, punishment, political control or administrative convenience.

Any health intervention affecting a person's liberty or autonomy must have a clearly stated basis and must be capable of review.

Where possible, a person shall participate meaningfully in decisions concerning their health.

Information concerning treatment shall be communicated in language the person can reasonably understand.

The constitutional principle is:

**Health care supports the person. It does not own the person.**

---

# Article 13 — Reablement

Reablement shall seek to increase capability.

Its purpose is not to create permanent dependency upon institutions.

Reablement may support a person's ability to:

- live independently;
- communicate;
- move safely;
- study;
- work;
- use technology;
- manage everyday activities;
- participate in community life;
- regain skills following disruption or illness.

A successful reablement system progressively transfers practical control back to the individual wherever possible.

**Support should increase what a person can do.**

---

# Article 14 — IWU: Law and Rights

IWU shall provide the lawful framework through which rights, duties and public decisions are administered.

No authority shall exercise significant power over another person without being capable of answering:

**What power are you using?**

**What law authorises it?**

**What evidence supports the decision?**

**Who is responsible for the decision?**

**How can the person challenge it?**

IWU shall require public authorities to distinguish clearly between evidence, allegation, professional opinion, risk assessment and final determination.

These categories shall not be treated as interchangeable.

---

# Article 15 — Notice and Reasons

A person adversely affected by a public decision shall ordinarily receive sufficient notice to understand what has occurred.

Where a decision affects housing, liberty, access to services, property, education or another significant interest, reasons shall be recorded.

The individual shall be able to identify the responsible authority.

Secret reasoning shall not become ordinary governance.

---

# Article 16 — Right to Challenge

The exercise of public power must be contestable.

A person may question a decision without being treated as hostile merely for doing so.

Complaints, appeals, legal challenges and requests for evidence are legitimate forms of civic participation.

No public authority shall retaliate against a person merely because that person exercised a lawful right of review.

**Accountability requires the possibility of disagreement.**

---

# Article 17 — IJI: Review and Balance

IJI shall examine situations in which:

- institutions disagree;
- evidence is contested;
- rights conflict;
- public power may have exceeded its lawful purpose;
- an individual alleges serious procedural failure;
- a decision creates substantial risk of injustice.

IJI shall not simply repeat the original decision.

Review must be capable of changing an outcome where evidence or procedure requires it.

---

# Article 18 — Independent Review

A review body must be sufficiently independent from the original decision-maker to examine that decision meaningfully.

The reviewer shall consider:

1. the facts relied upon;
2. the evidence supporting those facts;
3. the legal or constitutional authority being used;
4. whether the person was heard;
5. whether less restrictive alternatives existed;
6. whether the outcome was proportionate;
7. whether the decision remains justified.

Review exists to prevent power from becoming self-validating.

---

# Article 19 — Evidence Before Authority

Authority alone does not make a proposition true.

Within OBINexus, important decisions shall operate according to a verification principle:

**CLAIM → EVIDENCE → REVIEW → DECISION**

Where uncertainty remains, uncertainty must be acknowledged.

Where evidence conflicts, that conflict must be recorded.

Where a conclusion changes, the record should show why.

This principle connects civic governance with the wider OBINexus philosophy of verification-first systems.

---

# Article 20 — Separation of Powers

OHA, IWU and IJI shall not collapse into a single authority.

**OHA provides.**

**IWU governs the lawful exercise of power.**

**IJI reviews and balances that exercise of power.**

OHA cannot declare its own conduct lawful merely because it provided the service.

IWU cannot make itself immune from review merely because it interprets the rules.

IJI cannot itself become an unlimited authority.

Every institution remains bounded.

---

# Article 21 — The Person at the Centre

The purpose of constitutional architecture is not bureaucracy.

The purpose is the human being.

Where several institutions become involved in one person's life, administrative complexity shall not be transferred onto that person unnecessarily.

The system should coordinate around the individual rather than forcing the individual to repeatedly reconstruct their entire life story for each institution.

The person remains the subject of rights, not an object passed between departments.

---

# Article 22 — Constitutional Rule of OBINexus

The Republic of OBINexus therefore adopts the following civic sequence:

**HOME → HEALTH → REABLEMENT → PARTICIPATION**

supported by:

**OHA → IWU → IJI**

and governed throughout by:

**DIGNITY → EVIDENCE → ACCOUNTABILITY → AUTONOMY**

No house shall become a hospital merely because its resident requires support.

No health service shall become a housing authority merely because its patient requires accommodation.

No administrative authority shall become its own final judge.

No review body shall operate without limits.

And no human being shall disappear behind the machinery designed to serve them.

**The system exists for the person.  
The person does not exist for the system.**
